Key Empirical Insights & Scientific Findings

Strictly deduced from 115 years of meteorological records (1901 - 2015) across 36 subdivisions.

🌊 Southwest Monsoon Dominance

The Southwest Monsoon (Jun–Sep) constitutes 75.2% of India's annual rainfall nationally. In agricultural powerhouses like Bihar, Maharashtra, and Gujarat, monsoon rainfall represents over 85% of the total annual precipitation budget.

⛰️ Extreme Regional Disparity

There is a massive 11.7× precipitation disparity between India's wettest subdivision (Arunachal Pradesh: ~3,435 mm/yr) and the hyper-arid West Rajasthan (~292.7 mm/yr), driven by the orographic barrier of the Western Ghats and Himalayan foothills.

📅 Historic National Deluges & Droughts

The highest national deluge occurred in 1961 (1,717.1 mm), while the severest historical drought occurred during the 1972 El Niño crisis (1,146.5 mm), which caused acute agricultural deficits across Maharashtra, Gujarat, and Rajasthan.

🔮 Predicted 2016 Rainfall Outlook

The validated Ridge Regression model ($R^2 = 0.888$, $MAE = 215.56 ext{ mm}$) forecasts an All-India mean of 1,387.9 mm for 2016. This projects a recovery towards normal rainfall following back-to-back weak monsoon droughts in 2014 and 2015.
